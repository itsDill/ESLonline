📖 Key Vocabulary Review

Business Roles & Departments

Manager
A person who supervises and makes decisions for a team or department
Director
A senior leader responsible for a large area of the company
Administrator
A person who handles office tasks and organization
Executive
A senior manager with high responsibility and decision-making power
HR (Human Resources)
The department that handles hiring, training, and employee management
IT (Information Technology)
The department responsible for computers and technical systems
Finance
The department managing money, budgets, and accounting
Marketing
The department focused on promoting products and reaching customers

Business Communication Phrases

Nice to meet you
A polite greeting when meeting someone for the first time
What do you do?
A professional way to ask someone about their job
In my opinion...
A polite way to share your viewpoint in a meeting
I see your point, but...
A professional way to respectfully disagree
Can I help you?
An offer to provide assistance
I'm sorry, but I'm not sure
Politely expressing uncertainty
🔤 Grammar Focus Areas

Present Simple (Verb "to be")

Usage: Describe identity, occupation, nationality

Examples:

  • I am a project manager.
  • She is from Germany.
  • They are IT specialists.

Present Simple (Regular Verbs)

Usage: Describe habits, facts, regular duties

Examples:

  • We work with 50 countries.
  • He manages the sales team.
  • They provide customer support.

Past Simple

Usage: Describe completed past events and actions

Examples:

  • The company was founded in 1990.
  • She signed the contract last week.
  • I talked to the director yesterday.

Present Continuous

Usage: Describe actions happening right now

Examples:

  • She is presenting to the board now.
  • We are planning a new project.
  • They are working on the proposal.

Present Perfect

Usage: Describe experiences, recent events with relevance to now

Examples:

  • I have worked here for five years.
  • She has led many projects.
  • They haven't finished the report yet.

There is / There are

Usage: Describe existence of things

Examples:

  • There is a cafeteria on the ground floor.
  • Are there meeting rooms available?
  • There are five departments in our company.

Modals (Can, Could, Will, May)

Usage: Express ability, possibility, requests

Examples:

  • I can help you with that.
  • Could you send me the file?
  • We will expand next year.

Comparatives & Superlatives

Usage: Compare products, companies, features

Examples:

  • Our model is more efficient than the competitor's.
  • This is the most innovative product we've launched.
  • The service is better than before.
✏️ Practice Activities & Exercises

1. Vocabulary Matching Exercise

Objective: Match business terms with their definitions

How to Practice:

  • Review the vocabulary section above
  • Create flashcards for each term
  • Practice daily for 10 minutes
  • Test yourself after studying each unit

2. Grammar Fill-in-the-Blank

Objective: Master all tenses and structures

Practice Sentences:

  • He ___ (work) in the finance department since 2020.
  • There ___ (be) a meeting tomorrow at 2pm.
  • She ___ (sign) the contract last week.
  • We ___ (develop) new software right now.
  • Our service ___ (be) better than competitors.

3. Role-Play Scenarios

Objective: Practice professional conversations

Scenarios to Practice:

  • 🤝 Meet a colleague for the first time
  • 📞 Handle a customer complaint over the phone
  • 📋 Describe your job responsibilities
  • 💬 Share your opinion politely in a meeting
  • 🏨 Book a hotel for business travel
  • 📊 Present your company's history

4. Comprehension Passages

Objective: Improve reading and listening comprehension

Types of Passages:

  • Company descriptions and backgrounds
  • Job advertisements and descriptions
  • Customer service scenarios
  • Business meeting dialogues
  • Email communications

5. Speaking Practice Tasks

Objective: Build confidence in spoken English

Recording Topics:

  • Introduce yourself professionally (30 seconds)
  • Describe your company (1 minute)
  • Explain your job responsibilities (1 minute)
  • Compare two products (2 minutes)
  • Describe a business process (1-2 minutes)

💡 Tips for Test Success
1. Daily Practice

Spend 15-20 minutes daily reviewing vocabulary and grammar. Consistency is more important than long study sessions.

2. Active Recall

Test yourself on material without looking at notes. Try to remember vocabulary and grammar rules from memory.

3. Speaking Out Loud

Practice speaking conversations and role-plays aloud. This helps with pronunciation and memory retention.

4. Review Weak Areas

Identify which units or grammar structures are challenging and focus extra practice there.

5. Create Flashcards

Make digital or physical flashcards for vocabulary and key phrases. Review them regularly throughout the day.

6. Read Authentic Materials

Read business emails, company websites, and professional articles to see language in real contexts.

7. Mock Tests

Take practice tests under timed conditions to prepare for the actual Final Test.

8. Review Your Mistakes

When you make errors, understand why they're wrong and learn the correct form.
